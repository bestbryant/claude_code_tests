From Mass Spectrum to Molecule

Technical brief · October 18, 2026

How a top public model for Kaggle’s Enveda CASMI 2026 challenge works, and what our strategy has found so far

In short. A top public pipeline (reported 0.450) retrieves known structures of the right mass, scores them with several kinds of spectral evidence, fuses the rankings and breaks isomer ties with a spectrum-predicting model. Our tracks (Section 3): we forked it unchanged with its pinned software (A, score pending), learned that our novel-molecule generator adds at most ~0.01 (B), found two bugs where test and training data are processed differently (C) and are building a fully rule-compliant pipeline (D).

1The problem

In LC-MS/MS, liquid chromatography separates the sample; a tandem mass spectrometer then ionises each molecule, weighs the intact ion (the precursor m/z, mass-to-charge ratio), breaks it by gas collisions (20, 40 or 60 eV) and weighs the charged pieces, the fragments. A spectrum lists the (fragment m/z, intensity) peaks. Task: for each hidden molecule (natural-product-like, i.e. resembling molecules made by organisms; 1–16 spectra, median 3) return up to 25 ranked structures as SMILES (structures written as text, e.g. CCO = ethanol).

Why both? The precursor mass, precise to a few ppm, nearly fixes the molecular formula, but many structures share one formula (isomers, e.g. ethanol and dimethyl ether, both C2H6O). Only the fragment pattern, which depends on how the atoms are connected, tells them apart: shortlist by mass, rank by fragments.

Score, MRR@25 (mean reciprocal rank): mean over molecules of 1/rank of the first correct guess (rank 2 gives 0.5; outside the top 25, 0). Correct = same atoms bonded the same way (first block of the tautomer-canonical InChIKey: 3-D arrangement ignored, tautomers count as equal).

Data and rules. 2.54M training spectra of ~275,810 structures from 11 public libraries. Code runs offline in a Kaggle notebook (≤ 9 h); prize entries need public, free, commercially usable data (no NIST) and reproducible weights.

A noisy scoreboard. Of ~400 hidden molecules the public board uses ~124 (31%): one molecule moving from miss to rank 1 is worth ~0.008 (standard error ~0.016), so gaps of 1–2 molecules are noise. Final standings use the other 69% (private board). Oct 17: top 0.495, best public notebooks ~0.44–0.45, 3,486 teams.

Key terms

Da (dalton): ≈ mass of one H atom; for these singly charged ions m/z = mass in Da. ppm: parts per million (10 ppm of 400 Da = 0.004 Da).

Adduct: the charged form measured, e.g. [M+H]+ or [M+Na]+ (10 allowed); it converts precursor m/z to neutral mass M.

PubChem: public database of ~105M known structures; COCONUT: open natural-product database.

Fingerprint: one bit per substructure (present/absent). Tanimoto = bits ON in both ÷ bits ON in either (1 = identical, 0 = nothing shared).

Analog: a training molecule with a similar spectrum. LightGBM: boosted decision trees; LambdaRank trains them to put the right answer near the top of a list.

2How the current public model works

One of the strongest public notebooks (dmitriigluzdov, “From Spectra to Structures”, reports 0.450) stacks community work into a retrieve-then-rank pipeline (Fig. 1):

1Mass → candidates. M = median neutral mass over the spectra; candidates = all structures of a ~711k pool (training + COCONUT) within 10 ppm of M (30 ppm fallback): tens to hundreds, mostly isomers (Fig. 2).
2aEngine 1 (ahmedberatozer v4n), ~100 features per candidate:
  • library: peak-matching similarity (spectral entropy) to the candidate’s own reference spectra; handles most of class 1;
  • analog propagation: training molecules with similar spectra (peaks may be offset by their mass difference) vote for candidates that resemble them (Tanimoto);
  • FPNet: a neural network predicts from the peaks which substructures are present (the fingerprint); agreeing candidates score high;
  • fragmentation: peak intensity explained by breaking 1–2 bonds, plus a learned fragment scorer;
  • generator: chemical edits of the 6 closest spectral analogs (class 3).
LightGBM rankers combine the features; final score = z + 0.15 × popularity (z: score standardised per candidate list; popularity: log of PubChem substance + literature counts, since well-studied compounds are likelier answers).
2bPubChem channel, for class-2 molecules outside the pool: fingerprint models score PubChem structures (of ~105M) in the mass window (z + 0.25 × popularity). Top picks are interleaved as a hedge at ranks 2, 4, …, 10 (or 4, 8, …, 20), promoted to rank 1 if the fingerprint-score margin is large or the pick well documented, skipped given a library match ≥ 0.9.
2cEngine 2: an independent second opinion (own candidates, channels, two boosted rankers); combining two opinions reduces errors.
3Fusion: reciprocal-rank fusion (each list adds 1/(c + rank), c a constant); duplicates merged.
4Re-rank isomers: in each formula group, forward models ICEBERG/GLACIER predict each candidate’s spectrum; the best match to the measured one moves up (fragment score as fallback). Engine 2’s top pick goes to rank 2.

History: ~0.34 (mid Sep) → 0.40 (engine fusion) → 0.44 (forward models) → 0.45 (tuned PubChem promotion, deeper engine-2 trees); the last +0.01 ≈ one molecule, within noise.

Weak spots: almost no class-3 hits; many misses are the wrong isomer; tweaks accepted on the noisy board without cross-validation; some weights have non-commercial licences (barred for prizes) or unpublished construction.

3Our strategy (first session, Oct 17–18)

AGet on the board. Forked the 0.450 notebook unchanged on its fixed software versions (copies on newer ones lost ~0.04). Score pending.
BClass-3 deep-dive (the largest untapped share).
  • Proxy: 16,917 training structures are not in PubChem (all 106M keys checked): real measured molecules, a class-3 stand-in. 45% split at one amide/ester bond into two PubChem-known pieces (N-acyl amino acids, bile-acid conjugates, fatty-acid esters).
  • NovelEngine: two generators; both output only structures absent from PubChem, COCONUT and training.
    • Analog derivatives: find training spectra similar to the query by modified cosine (which also matches peaks shifted by the mass difference Δm), then edit the analogs’ structures with transformations (hydroxylation, methylation, glycosylation, chain extension, …) whose mass change equals Δm.
    • Conjugates (motivated by the 45%): known acid + known amine/alcohol joined by an amide/ester bond (or glycoside), whose masses minus the water lost on bond formation equal M; ranked by fragment ions typical of each half.
  • Site localisation (our new idea, Fig. 3) scores where on the analog the edit sits; a learned ranker (LambdaRank) re-orders the candidates (results: Section 4).
  • Reality check: only-novel candidates scored 0.010 on the public board (about one molecule at rank 1) vs ~0.045 predicted from the proxy (Fig. 5): reach was overstated ~4–5×, so our class-3 engine adds at most ~0.01. Generating is far harder than retrieving: even on the proxy, analog derivatives reached the right structure for only 24% of molecules.
CBug hunt on the public pipeline. Two train/serve mismatches (a feature computed one way in training, another way at test time, so learned rules misfire): (1) number of peaks: raw test spectra (median 230) vs cleaned training ones (median 52); (2) positive ions lack one electron (0.000548 Da): hidden-test m/z values are corrected for it, but the enveda-180 training spectra used to fit all models are not, so mass-error features are off by ~1.6 ppm at the median mass, two-thirds of the ~2.5 ppm typical gap between formulas (Fig. 2). Two patched variants: pending.
DCompliance-first pipeline of our own (all code and weights ours, reproducible, permitted data).
  • Pool of 726,198 structures (training + COCONUT Oct-2026). Channels: library entropy similarity, analog propagation, fragment explanation, COCONUT documentation priors (organisms, literature, synonyms) and our own model.
  • Dual encoder (Fig. 4): learns to place the true structure closer than up to 64 same-mass “hard negatives” (contrastive loss) and to predict fingerprint bits. Its held-out score (Sec. 4) is from an easier task than the leaderboard (the answer is always in the list): not comparable with 0.450. Longer runs in progress.
  • Ranker: LambdaRank on simulated queries from 8,000 training structures (class 1: some of a structure’s spectra are the query, the rest stay in the library; class 2: all removed), validated on 1,500 disjoint structures (250 Enveda natural products measured like the test, 750 other natural products, 500 others), using the measured mass (the true one would leak the answer) and electron correction.

Takeaways
  • A proxy is a hypothesis about the test: validate it against the leaderboard early. The board is noisy (±0.016), so offline validation that mimics the test is also needed.
  • With current methods the reachable points are in classes 1–2: rank the right isomer first.

5Next steps (track E)

Compare variants on the board; finish our own pipeline (target ≥ 0.45, fully compliant); add the class-3 engine only where the main list is weak; choose the two submissions scored on the private board by validation + board. Why: hosts “reserve the right to check all submissions”; if enforced, strong compliant entries may outrank non-reproducible ones.

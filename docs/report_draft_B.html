From Mass Spectrum to Molecule

How the current public model for the Enveda CASMI 2026 Kaggle challenge works, and what our strategy has found so far

Technical brief · October 18, 2026 · competition runs Sep 14 – Dec 14 2026 ($50k, top 5 paid)
In short. The best public pipeline (public score 0.450) is a retrieval system: compute the unknown molecule’s mass, list every known structure with that mass, score each one with several kinds of spectral evidence, fuse the rankings, and break ties between same-formula isomers with a model that predicts spectra. We (A) reproduced it, (B) attacked the “never-seen molecule” share and learned it is worth at most ~0.01, (C) found two train/serve bugs in it, and (D) are building a fully rule-compliant pipeline of our own.

1The problem

A tandem mass spectrometer (LC-MS/MS) turns a molecule into a charged ion, weighs it (the precursor m/z, mass-to-charge ratio), then breaks it with gas collisions (20, 40 or 60 eV) and weighs the pieces. The resulting spectrum is a list of (fragment m/z, intensity) peaks plus the precursor m/z. Each hidden test molecule (natural-product-like) comes with 1–16 spectra (median 3) from a Bruker timsTOF, possibly at several energies and adducts. The task: return up to 25 ranked structure guesses (SMILES strings).

Score: MRR@25 (mean reciprocal rank) = average over molecules of 1/rank of the first correct guess: rank 1 → 1, rank 2 → 0.5, not in top 25 → 0. “Correct” = same atom connectivity (first InChIKey block; stereochemistry ignored).

Three kinds of test molecule (true shares hidden; community estimates from leaderboard probes):

  • Class 1 (~16–28%): public reference spectra of the structure exist → library look-up works (MRR ~0.93).
  • Class 2 (~20–45%): structure is in a database (PubChem or COCONUT) but was never measured → retrieve it and rank it above same-mass rivals.
  • Class 3 (~39–52%): new structure, not in PubChem → must be generated.

Data and rules. 2.54M training spectra of ~275,810 structures from 11 public libraries (largest: enveda-180, 1.15M timsTOF spectra of drug-like compounds; enveda-np-examples: 250 natural products measured like the test). Code runs offline in a Kaggle notebook (≤ 9 h). For prizes, data and pretrained weights must be public, free, commercially usable and reproducible from public code (no NIST).

A noisy scoreboard. The hidden test has ~400 molecules; the public leaderboard sees only 31% (~124). One molecule moving from miss to rank 1 changes the public score by ~0.008 (standard error ~0.016). On Oct 17: top 0.495, best public notebooks 0.44–0.45, 3,486 teams.

Key terms
Adduct
the charged form actually measured, e.g. [M+H]+ (molecule + proton) or [M+Na]+; ten are allowed. Knowing it turns precursor m/z into the neutral mass M.
Fragment
a charged piece of the broken ion; each one gives a peak.
Isomers
different structures with the same formula, hence the same mass: mass alone cannot separate them.
Fingerprint
a bit vector; each bit says whether a given substructure is present. Tanimoto = share of bits two molecules have in common.
Analog
a training molecule whose spectrum resembles the query; usually a close structural relative.
Forward model
predicts the spectrum of a given structure (the reverse direction of our task).
ppm
parts per million of the mass: 10 ppm of a 400 Da molecule is 0.004 Da.

2How the current public model works

The strongest public notebook (“From Spectra to Structures”, v51, public 0.450; community “E006” lineage) stacks many community contributions (Fig. 1). It can only output structures that are in its databases, plus a few from a small generator.

  1. Mass → candidates. M = median neutral mass over the spectra. Every structure within 10 ppm of M (30 ppm fallback) in a ~711k pool (training structures + COCONUT natural products) is a candidate: typically tens to hundreds, mostly isomers (Fig. 2).
  2. Evidence channels (Engine 1). Each candidate gets ~100 features:
    • library: spectral-entropy similarity of the query to the candidate’s own reference spectra (solves class 1);
    • analog propagation: training spectra that resemble the query (after a mass shift) vote for candidates structurally similar to their molecules (Tanimoto);
    • fingerprint model FPNet: a transformer reads peaks + metadata and predicts the unknown’s substructure bits; candidates with matching fingerprints score high;
    • fragmentation: share of peak intensity explained by breaking 1–2 bonds of the candidate, plus a learned fragment scorer;
    • a small class-3 generator (chemical transformations of the 6 nearest analogs).
    Four LightGBM gradient-boosted models combine the features; then a popularity prior is added: z(score) + 0.15 × log(PubChem substance + literature counts). Top 60 kept.
  3. PubChem channel. Fingerprint models screen ~105M PubChem structures in the mass window (z + 0.25 × popularity). Picks are inserted at ranks 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, promoted to rank 1 when the fingerprint margin is large (> 150) or the compound is well documented, and skipped when a strong library match (similarity ≥ 0.9) exists.
  4. Engine 2. Independent candidates (also ChEBI, LIPID MAPS), own channels, two gradient-boosted rankers blended 0.88/0.12; top 40.
  5. Fusion + forward models. Reciprocal-rank fusion (each list adds 1/(constant + rank)) after merging duplicates. Inside each formula group, ICEBERG/GLACIER predict every candidate’s spectrum; the best match to the measured spectrum moves up (fragment score if they give no output). Engine 2’s top pick goes to rank 2.

History: 0.34 (mid Sep, 4-channel retrieval + ranker) → 0.40 (engine fusion) → 0.44 (forward-model re-ranking) → 0.45 (tuned PubChem promotion, deeper Engine-2 trees).

Weak spots: almost no class-3 hits; many misses are the wrong isomer; tweaks were accepted on the noisy 124-molecule board without cross-validation; licence/reproducibility of several bundled weights is uncertain (some CC-BY-NC-SA, one ranker file of unpublished construction).

3Our strategy (first session, Oct 17–18)

AGet on the board. Forked the 0.450 notebook unchanged, keeping its pinned Docker image (teams that ran copies on a newer image lost ~0.04). Score pending.
BClass-3 deep-dive (the largest untapped share).
  • A proxy for “novel”. Checking all 106M PubChem connectivity keys found 16,917 training structures that are not in PubChem: real measured molecules, the closest stand-in for class 3. 45% of them split at one amide/ester bond into two PubChem-known pieces (N-acyl amino acids, bile-acid conjugates, fatty-acid esters).
  • NovelEngine, two generators that output only structures absent from PubChem, COCONUT and training: (i) analog derivatives: find similar training spectra by modified cosine (also pairs peaks shifted by the mass difference Δm), then apply transformations (hydroxylation, methylation, glycosylation, chain extension, …) whose mass change equals Δm; from the top-20 analogs this reaches 24% of the proxy; (ii) conjugates: join a known acid to a known amine/alcohol (amide/ester/glycoside) whose masses add up, ranked by diagnostic fragments of each half.
  • Site localisation (our new scoring idea, Fig. 3) raised derivative MRR on the proxy from 0.061 to 0.107; a learned LambdaRank ranker raised proxy MRR from 0.124 to 0.269.
  • Reality check. A submission of only novel candidates scored 0.010 public vs ~0.045 predicted from the proxy: the proxy overstates reach ~4–5×, and class 3 is worth at most ~0.01 as an add-on. A “known-only” run of the same engine scored 0.085.
CBug hunt on the public pipeline (code review by a subagent) found two train/serve mismatches: (1) the ranker feature “number of peaks” is computed on raw test peaks (median 230) but was trained on cleaned peaks (median 52); (2) all models were fitted on enveda-180 masses without the electron-mass correction (−0.000548 Da) that the hidden test’s positive-mode m/z values carry: a ~1.6 ppm shift in mass-error features at the median mass, against a ~2.5 ppm typical gap between competing formulas (Fig. 2). Two patched variants submitted (scores pending).
DCompliance-first pipeline of our own (prize eligibility: all code and weights ours, reproducible, permitted data).
  • Pool: 726,198 structures (training + COCONUT Oct-2026 release).
  • Channels: library entropy similarity, analog propagation, fragment explanation, COCONUT documentation priors (organisms, literature, synonyms), and our own spectrum↔structure model.
  • Own model, a dual encoder (Fig. 4): it learns to place a spectrum and its true structure close together and up to 64 same-mass “hard negatives” far away (contrastive loss), while also predicting fingerprint bits. After one CPU epoch it ranks held-out structures among their same-mass candidates with MRR 0.51 (top-1 36%); longer CPU and GPU runs are in progress.
  • Ranker: LambdaRank (a LightGBM model trained to order lists) on simulated queries from 8,000 training structures (class 1: hide only the query spectra; class 2: hide every spectrum of the structure), validated on 1,500 disjoint structures (250 enveda-np-examples, 750 natural products, 500 others). Simulations use the measured mass (the true mass would leak the answer) and apply the electron correction to mimic the test.

5Next steps (track E)

Compare the variants on the board; finish our own pipeline (target ≥ 0.45 while fully compliant); add the class-3 engine only where the main list is weak; pick the two final submissions by validation + board. Why: if the hosts enforce the rules (they “reserve the right to check all submissions”), strong compliant entries may outrank non-reproducible ones.

### Dropped by the critic

- **7. Contrastive fine-tuning of GLACIER with our pool isomers as decoys** -- It would destroy GLACIER's main asset. GLACIER is our least population-biased scorer (+0.019 excess) precisely because it was never contrastively trained against our pool. Using COCONUT-not-train, PubChemLite and ChEBI/LMSD as same-formula decoys while all positives are train.parquet structures teaches 'COCONUT-not-train never produces the spectrum'. That is the exact mechanism that gave pf_bern its +0.137 excess and cost us the COCONUT probe. Using training-structure decoys only would avoid this, but then the remaining expected gain is the MCES-disjoint +1.4 top-1 points, i.e. LB-invisible. Cost also does not fit: 7-10 engineering days, DGL on a Kaggle T4, 15-25 of roughly 70 remaining T4-h, plus a higher class-1 cost (-0.014 already). It also overlaps item 3, which distils GLACIER into stage 1.
- **6. Late-interaction peak-to-atom MaxSim head (FLARE-style)** -- It is the most expensive item: 6-8 engineering days plus 12-16 TPU-h, competing with items 3 and PF-x for the same TPU and the same 0.044 COCONUT gap. Its fate is tied to item 3. If spectral gains do not transfer to the hidden COCONUT answers (the Oct 26 specpfb vs specpfd reading), MaxSim will not transfer either. If item 3 succeeds, MaxSim's marginal value over a GLACIER-distilled PeakFormer shrinks. The FLARE anchor is MassSpecGym with an oracle formula against weak 2024 baselines, not an f.z model already trained on 2.5M spectra with window negatives. A new GCN atom encoder trained on training structures is also another route for the population prior. Revisit only if TPU hours sit idle after PF-x and item 3.

### Missing from the synthesis (critic)

- Compliance as an actual work item, not a footnote. With 0.339 eligible against a 0.504 public top, the only realistic prize path is that higher-ranked teams turn out ineligible, so a clean reproducibility record is the asset. Needed: a LICENSE file (owner's decision, still missing); public training kernels for PF-b, FP-b, PF-x, the stage-1 and stage-2 rankers, the pool / srckeys / Enamine-tier builds and the vendor files; and one end-to-end rebuild from public inputs before the freeze. Budget 2-3 days and schedule it, or everything else is moot.
- The PF-x scale-up and a small PeakFormer ensemble (2-3 seeds or variants, mean f.z) have no shortlist entry. They are the direct continuation of the only change that has moved the LB since Oct 20 (PF-b: validation plateaued, capacity-bound). They need no new engineering, already run on the TPU, and should double as item 3's matched control. Expected +0.002 to +0.006, with low risk.
- Cheap conditional levers already pre-registered for the Oct 26 readings are missing. (1) A slot-keeping COCONUT order donor (v17b lists with the COCONUT-not-train slots re-ordered by PeakFormer b) if specpfb-coco >= probe17b-coco + 0.010. This is post-processing for 1 slot, and it directly attacks the 0.044 COCONUT gap. (2) RRF fusion of engines with different spectral evidence as the pick-#2 hedge.
- The never-run ZEN probe: direct spectral match (casmi/zen.py, already written) against the 3,717 Zenodo-filtered structures plus the unfiltered-only structures absent from train. This is 1 slot and half a day. It tells whether any hidden answers have Enveda spectra outside train. If they do, that is a library-precision channel (rank-1 insertion at similarity >= 0.7), with none of the misfire problems of PeakFormer-gated insertions. That is better use of item 2b's download than more Enamine training data.
- Calibration of component gates to LB detectability. PF-b's +0.124 neutral win rate produced +0.005 LB, and one public molecule is worth 0.0076. Gates of +0.02 to +0.03 (items 1, 3, 6, 7) therefore predict LB effects below the detection floor. Either raise the gates, or pre-register a policy to ship a BUNDLE of offline-gated changes and judge the bundle once on the LB plus the COCONUT and train probes. Testing each change separately will read noise.
- A generic population-balance invariant for every contrastively trained scorer (items 3, 6, 7, any FP or DreaMS variant). Each structure population must appear as positive and as negative in matched proportions within each input look, and in-batch negatives must be masked accordingly. This has been the single most damaging failure mode in this project; it should be a reviewed checklist item, not something rediscovered per model.
- Engineering capacity. The shortlist sums to about 33-42 engineering days, against about 30 working days before the freeze week. On top of that, container restarts kill local jobs and TPU queue waits run to hours. Cut to about 4 active lines, and keep every chain restartable.
- Base-list drift. The Oct 24 drift check found that only 122 of 400 v5w lists kept their full order under current code. Every new kernel needs a top-1 / list diff against its paired base, or post-processing A/Bs are not actually paired and their SE is closer to 0.010 than 0.005.
- Final-notebook runtime and hardware. Test at 2,500 spectra with the CE choice, a PeakFormer ensemble, item 5's network and possibly FRIGID together. Decide early whether the final notebook is CPU or GPU: a GPU notebook spends scarce T4 quota on every rerun, and the stack of 9 h limits gets tighter.
- Final-pick strategy. Decide explicitly whether pick #2 is an eligible hedge (base vs base + bundle, or RRF) and never an ineligible fork, given that the prize is the reason for the eligibility constraint. Do not let late single LB reads of 0.005 or less choose between them (winner's curse about 0.003-0.005).

### Kept (critic notes)

- Item 3, PF-syn, is the main bet but needs fixes before it starts. Verdict: the only idea that attacks the measured ordering loss among same-formula COCONUT isomers at its source. Cost is realistic: about 74 thread-hours of GLACIER simulation fits about 20 Kaggle CPU-kernel hours, and 16-20 TPU-h fits the quota if the TPU queue cooperates (the TPU probe kernel sat in the queue for hours on Oct 25). Eligibility is clean if the simulation and build scripts are published. Three corrections. (1) The gain estimate is inflated. Our only anchor is PF-b: it raised the neutral win rate vs training decoys by +0.124 (.667 to .791) and gave +0.005 LB, which is inside one retrain SE. The pre-registered gate of +0.03 win rate therefore maps to about +0.001 LB, which cannot be detected. Require at least +0.06 vs the matched control before spending the 8 TPU-h full run and an LB pair. Central expectation +0.003 to +0.006, not +0.008. (2) Population-prior leakage, the failure that cost pf_bern +0.137 excess, comes back through two doors the proposal leaves open. First, a 'real/synthetic' source token set to 'real' at inference is the instrument-token failure again, because real positives are 100% training structures. Second, the XLA trainer adds in-batch negatives (scripts/train_pf_xla.py), so synthetic COCONUT positives would become negatives for real spectra while never being real positives. Invariant: within each look (real or synthetic), every structure population must appear as both positive and negative in matched proportions. Real samples contrast only against training structures (mask synthetic rows out of their in-batch negatives); synthetic samples contrast against the full simulated window. Use no source token, or full token dropout. (3) The matched control should be the PF-x scale-up already in flight on TPU, not PF-b, so the control is itself a shippable model. Precondition stays: Oct 26 specpfb-coco - specpfd-coco >= 0. If that comes out negative, demote item 3 below items 1 and 4.
- Item 2 has a misread premise. Fold it into item 3's data build at zero marginal cost and give it no slot, no dedicated run and no expected gain. The cited -0.021 (casmi26-s2s-np-eu) shifted the TEST side of a pipeline whose mass-window, ppm and fragment-tolerance features use theory-exact masses. It says nothing about PeakFormer training data. PeakFormer already trains with 3 ppm per-spectrum calibration jitter plus 2 ppm per-peak jitter (scripts/train_pf.py:163-164). That is larger than the 0.55 mDa offset (1.8 ppm at m/z 300), and neutral-loss tokens do not see the offset at all. Expected gain is about 0. Apply the correction because it is correct, and skip the 1 h sensitivity test unless the CPU is idle (a 0.005 MRR threshold on 620 queries is at noise level anyway). Item 2b is better used differently, see 'missing': first run the never-submitted ZEN probe. Adding about 2,900 Enamine structures seen only as in-source-fragment adducts to PeakFormer training mostly adds more Enamine chemistry (already 46% of the data) and teaches little about natural-product water-loss spectra.
- Item 1, reduced to about 1-1.5 days. Correct mechanism, small ceiling. GLACIER as a whole is worth +0.010 LB, and the proxies touch about 15 molecules (about 5 public), so realistic total gain is at most +0.003. Changes: (a) For CE, pick ONE global offset, not the max over the grid. A max over 4 offsets lifts decoys as much as truths and washes out discrimination. (b) Gate on the npx timsTOF natural-product / COCONUT stratum, not the 0.838 enveda-180 harness, which is Enamine chemistry where GLACIER is already strong; that is not where the loss is. (c) The per-adduct split is another stage-2 feature, a category that has been neutral on the LB. Ship it only bundled. (d) Build the proxies only if the harness passes, and drop 1d entirely until the hosts answer about the checkpoint. The real value is infrastructure: the CE-correct GLACIER runner is reused by item 3's simulator, so do it first in the week.
- Item 4: keep as a strictly gated, one-shot test. Expected value is about +0.0015, given P(success) about 0.3. The one cheap thing worth learning is whether PF-b's misfire rate on the 206 npx timsTOF present queries falls from 29% toward the 8% non-timsTOF level. If it does not, close the 'other' line immediately with 0 LB slots spent. Two added risks. First, GLACIER cosine margins are not comparable across chemotypes: Enamine amides fragment predictably and get higher predicted-spectrum cosines than natural-product glycosides or terpenoids. The second key can therefore misfire in exactly the natural-product-present case that killed the earlier attempts, so calibrate delta on natural-product present queries only. Second, offline policy simulation failed to reproduce the hidden present-list cost (calib_policy.py). Before the A/B, submit a 'fired-only' probe: the promoted proposal at rank 1 for gated molecules, empty lists elsewhere. Its score x 124 gives the gate's hidden hit count, i.e. its precision measured on hidden data instead of simulated. Then one paired A/B, and stop for good on a result <= 0.
- Item 5: keep only the 0.5-day density check, then build conditionally. This is one of the few ideas that brings genuinely new information (test-to-test relations) rather than reweighting evidence we already have. 'Extra features on the same evidence' is exactly what keeps failing on the LB, so this is a real point in its favour. Two caveats. First, enveda-np-examples is a curated set of 250 molecules and may not show the hidden set's family density. A better read is an in-kernel restricted probe: v17b lists kept only for molecules that have a transform-linked test neighbour, empty elsewhere. It measures how much of our score sits on networked molecules, on the hidden set itself, for 1 slot. Second, propagate only from neighbours with lib_max >= 0.9 (class-1 precision about 0.93). Neighbours confident through stage-2 margins are wrong too often, and NAP-style propagation of wrong hits is the downside. Expected 0 to +0.006.
- Item 8: keep only the CPU step (0.5 day: bin the Tanimoto of thresholded PeakFormer morgan2 predictions to the truth), placed last. An added reason for scepticism: the PeakFormer Bernoulli head is trained with BCE on positives only, so it keeps the training-structure bit prior (plan.md, Population prior section). Conditioning FRIGID on it steers generation toward Enamine-like chemistry, the wrong prior for natural-product class 3. Also, a FRIGID scoring step needs a GPU in the final notebook, which moves the whole 9 h notebook onto GPU quota. Park class 3 unless the CPU bin shows at least 25% of queries at Tanimoto >= 0.5 AND item 3 has freed engineering time.

An independent re-implementation (MSAlign) got lower FLARE numbers: 28.4 Hit@1 on their MCES split, with oracle formula.
- Fit: PeakFormer already produces per-peak token states (exact m/z sinusoids plus neutral losses). Add a small GNN atom encoder and a MaxSim head, train with our same-mass-window negatives, and use it as a top-200 re-scorer and a LambdaRank feature. This keeps working without a known formula. Optionally add candidate-conditioned subformula tokens, i.e. annotate peaks under each candidate's own formula, which is legal. TPU-friendly if peaks are padded to 64 and atoms to 80 with a dense-adjacency GCN (static shapes). The pair cost is a 64×80 similarity matrix, which is cheap. Expect it to complement the FP and global-embedding scores; it targets class 2 and near-analog class 1.

### [retrieval-sota] Candidate-set InfoNCE on frozen foundation embeddings, plus late score fusion

- Source: Krzakala et al., 'MSAlign: Aligning Molecular and Mass Spectra Foundation Models for Metabolite Identification' (arXiv 2605.19752, NeurIPS 2026; code github.com/KrzakalaPaul/MSAlign-NeurIPS2026); De Waele et al., 'Small molecule retrieval from tandem mass spectrometry: what are we optimizing for?' (arXiv 2602.16507)
- What: Spectrum embeddings (DreaMS, or binned spectra) and molecule embeddings (Morgan FP, or MolDeBERTa) are frozen and precomputed. Light MLP adapters are trained with InfoNCE whose negatives are K candidates from the query's own candidate set, not in-batch. Several such aligners plus Gaussian mass scorers are fused by a convex combination tuned for validation R@1. De Waele et al. show theoretically and empirically that fingerprint accuracy and retrieval trade off.
- Evidence: MSAlign formula split, R@1: candidate-based InfoNCE 47.5 vs in-batch InfoNCE 19.5 vs regression 16.7 (ms→mol) / 22.8 (mol→ms). Single pairs: binned/FP 34.3, DreaMS/FP 38.6, binned/MolDeBERTa 44.5, DreaMS/MolDeBERTa 47.5. Fusing four aligners adds 6.9, mass scorers add 3.8, and both together add 10.7 (58.2). On the MCES split: 19.0, fused 30.8 (JESTR 7.9; MIST 16.4 with oracle formula). Errors of the different scorers overlap little (low Jaccard). Training takes under 2 min per epoch on a V100. De Waele et al. on MassSpecGym formula track, R@1: contrastive embedding-cosine 13.62 vs BCE 10.08 vs IoU loss 6.48. IoU loss has the best Tanimoto but the worst retrieval.
- Fit: Our PeakFormer already uses window negatives, which this confirms is the right loss. The cheap new part: DreaMS embeddings are already computed for our analog channel. Train 2-4 small adapters aligning DreaMS and binned spectra to our 15k-bit FP or Morgan, and optionally to a second molecular embedding, with window InfoNCE. That takes minutes on CPU or T4 and yields decorrelated stage-1 features; LambdaRank then does the fusion. If a SMILES language model is used (MolDeBERTa), RDKit-canonicalize every SMILES from every source first (see the artefact audit item), and check its pretraining data for eligibility. Graph or FP molecule encoders avoid both issues. Candidate-set training also learns a 'measured-molecule-likeness' prior. That prior is legitimate in this competition, since the pools come from databases.

### [retrieval-sota] Rank with a merged consensus spectrum (aggregate-then-rank) and train it as its own view

- Source: Chen & Hassoun, 'Learning from All Views: A Multiview Contrastive Framework for Metabolite Annotation' (MVP; Analytical Chemistry 2026, DOI 10.1021/acs.analchem.5c05675; bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.11.12.688047; code github.com/HassounLab/MVP)
- What: Four views (molecular graph, fingerprint, individual spectrum, consensus spectrum) are aligned with pairwise contrastive losses. The consensus spectrum is the union of peaks from all spectra of the same compound (all CEs and adducts); peaks with the same formula keep the maximum intensity. At inference, the consensus query is ranked against the molecule graph, instead of averaging per-spectrum ranks.
- Evidence: MassSpecGym Hit@1, individual spectra vs consensus: mass track 26.37 → 35.99; formula track 11.10 → 13.96 (MCES@1 11.61 → 10.37). Rank-then-aggregate is clearly worse: CFM-ID 4 averaging three CE spectra gets 2.00% Hit@1 vs MVP consensus 13.91% on 3,004 merged queries (14.38% with all spectra). Training only on [M+H]+ improves mol-s to 29.73% on the mass track. Removing the fingerprint view costs about 4 points. The mass-track numbers inherit ground-truth formula subformula labels, so the formula-track gain (+2.9 points, +26% relative) is the clean estimate.
- Fit: The hidden test has about 3.75 spectra per molecule. If PeakFormer, FP and GLACIER currently score per spectrum and then aggregate scores, switch the primary query to the merged consensus spectrum, and train consensus↔structure pairs as an explicit view or anchor. Our 'merge aug' may already cover part of this, so check how inference aggregates. For GLACIER, compare per-CE predictions to the matching-CE spectra, then sum, rather than comparing to an average. Cheap to do (no new model), and it affects all classes.

### [retrieval-sota] Per-candidate formula plausibility (learned formula ranker / subformula explanation) to separate isobars in the mass window

- Source: Audit arXiv 2606.19624, Table 2 and Table 7 (data-safe MIST-CF released in MassSpecGym v1.5, github.com/pluskal-lab/MassSpecGym); Goldman et al., MIST-CF (JCIM 2023); FRIGID arXiv 2604.16648 App. A.4
- What: MIST-CF is an energy-based formula transformer (2 layers, hidden 128). It scores each candidate formula from the SIRIUS mass decomposition by how well that formula's subformula assignments explain the MS2 peaks, with adduct and neutral-loss covariates. On a mass track, a per-candidate formula score acts as a soft formula filter.
- Evidence: MassSpecGym mass track: filtering candidates to the ground-truth formula alone lifts random Hit@1 from 0.43% to 12.98% (@20: 7.99 → 71.33). MIST with the data-safe MIST-CF top-1 formula gets 26.64%; with the ground-truth formula, 51.45%. So formula knowledge is worth about 25 Hit@1 points for a formula-aware model. Data-safe MIST-CF formula accuracy is 50.5% top-1 and 73.2% top-5; test-leaked versions get 59.0-66.5%. FRIGID stays reasonably robust when given the top-5 MIST-CF formulas instead of the oracle formula.
- Fit: Our pools are mass windows, so several formulas compete. Add features ranked within each window: (i) a MIST-CF-style formula energy for the candidate's formula, retrained on train.parquet from the MassSpecGym v1.5 data-safe code (small model, feasible on T4 or TPU); (ii) a cheap no-training version: intensity-weighted fraction of peaks explained by subformulas of the candidate formula at timsTOF tolerance, the number of unexplained top-k peaks, and element-count consistency (N/S/halogen fragments). Use MS1 isotope features if the test parquet exposes them. Check overlap with the existing 'fragment features' first. Gains will mostly show up where windows mix formulas (class 2 and class 1).

### [retrieval-sota] Calibration: benchmark artefacts and shortcuts that inflate reported retrieval gains (which literature numbers to trust)

- Source: Liu, Bushuiev, Lightheart et al. (MIT/IOCB/Enveda/Tufts), 'MassSpecGym in the Wild' (arXiv 2606.19624, Jun 2026), which releases MassSpecGym v1.5; SpecBridge arXiv 2601.17204 (withdrawn); Gupta & Skinnider, bioRxiv 2026.05.03.722532 (property-matched decoys)
- What: An audit of 26 MassSpecGym papers found evaluation problems in at least 17. The issues: SMILES-canonicalization mismatch (true SMILES non-canonical, decoys RDKit-canonical); PubChem ranking and popularity bias in the candidate sets; ground-truth formula leaking into the mass track; leaky simulators and formula annotators; and a MIST padding-mask bug ('attn += bool_mask'). v1.5 fixes canonicalization and ships data-safe MIST-CF, ICEBERG and DreaMS.
- Evidence: Corrected numbers: DreaMS+ChemBERTa alignment (SpecBridge-like) 82.41 → 6.70; ChemFormer two-stage retrieval (GLMR-like) 63.89 → 7.60; a ChemBERTa classifier on SMILES syntax only 99.66 → 8.2. A spectrum-blind PubChem default order gets 49.98% Hit@1 (mass track); ranking by number of chiral atoms gets 4.88%, roughly the FP-FFN level. The batching bug inflates MIST Tanimoto from 0.457 to 0.812 and MIST+MolForge top-1 from 10.73% to 31.75%. SpecBridge (claimed 84.7%) was withdrawn. Clean formula-track hierarchy (Hit@1): GLACIER 49.9 > ICEBERG 2.0 44.4 > MARASON 34.0 > FLARE 22.7 > MVP 14.0 > JESTR 11.8 > MIST 9.6 > FP-FFN 5.1 > random 3.1.
- Fit: (1) Don't spend weeks on SpecBridge, GLMR or other SMILES-language-model retrievers; the real gains are forward models, synthetic augmentation, late interaction, candidate negatives and consensus. (2) Popularity and order priors are a huge, legitimate signal here (likely what powers the public lineage's 'other' channel). Keep them as ranker features, and on HL-val run a constant-spectrum ablation to quantify how much of each source's MRR is prior vs spectrum. (3) RDKit-canonicalize all SMILES, dedupe by InChIKey14 across train/COCONUT/PubChemLite, and never feed raw source SMILES strings to a learned model, since format differs by source (a hidden 'source rule'). (4) Check that PeakFormer's attention masks use -inf and that validation batches are not grouped by molecule. Pure diagnostics, essentially free.

### [forward-models] GLACIER (single-stage forward model): our current reranker and the cheap tweaks still open

- Source: Wang, Wang & Coley, 'GLACIER: Rethinking Mass Spectrum Prediction as an Object Detection Problem', arXiv 2606.29161 (2026). Code: github.com/coleygroup/ms-pred (MIT; local checkout /home/user/ext/mspred/ms-pred at commit 708148c). Newer MSG checkpoint: Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.23303289 (glacier_msg.ckpt, MIT, 2026-10-20; loads only through the mist.glacier compatibility loader in github.com/coleygroup/MIST). Public LB deltas: kaggle.com/code/alexchilton/casmi26-public-lineage-0-34-to-0-44 and kaggle.com/code/dariushafshar/casmi26-the-public-chain-version-by-version. Our results: docs/plan.md.
- What: A Graphormer encoder predicts bond breakpoints, so all fragments come out of one GPU pass with no CPU enumeration. A transformer decoder then predicts an intensity for each exact-mass fragment, conditioned on adduct, collision energy and instrument. Candidates are ranked by the entropy or cosine similarity between each candidate's predicted spectrum and the query spectrum.
- Evidence: MassSpecGym (MSG) retrieval, up to 256 candidates, recall@1. Mass challenge: 69.7% (no contrastive fine-tuning) and 70.0% (with), vs ICEBERG 2.0 63.95, FraGNNet 46.6, NEIMS 8.4. Formula challenge: 48.5 and 49.9, vs ICEBERG 2.0 44.4, MARASON 34.0, FraGNNet 31.9, NEIMS 7.6.
NIST20 top-1: random split 0.390 / 0.525 (no CF / CF), vs ICEBERG 2.0 0.335 and MassFormer 0.212. Scaffold split 0.384 / 0.460.
MSG cosine 0.58 vs ICEBERG 0.47. Speed 1.3 ms per spectrum (368.7 spectra/s per A5000) vs ICEBERG 10.8 ms (7.95x).
Caveat: the MSG numbers come from the collision-energy-annotated subset. On the full MSG test set with imputed energies, ICEBERG drops to 36.18% formula-bonus R@1 (MassSpecGym-in-the-Wild Table 3).
Our pipeline:
- Within-formula isomer top-1: GLACIER 0.838 (own runner) vs our FP encoder 0.568. FP+GLACIER 0.804, FP+analog+GLACIER+ICEBERG 0.870 (public runner).
- Stage-2 CV 0.825 -> 0.840. LB 0.307 -> 0.317.
- COCONUT queries +0.033/+0.040, class 1 -0.014.
- Coverage 371/400 molecules. CPU speed 8.6 predictions/s/thread.
Public lineage:
- ICE+GLACIER rescoring of the fused top 40: 0.382 -> 0.386.
- GLACIER restricted to [M+H]+ spectra: 0.409 -> 0.413, and +0.006 in v29.
- Forward weight set to 0 when lib_max >= 0.9: 0.404 -> 0.404.
- Fit: Gap: COCONUT / class-2 isomer discrimination. It is already in stage 2. Remaining one-variable experiments:
- (a) We feed both [M+H]+ and [M+Na]+ to GLACIER, but the public line gained +0.004 to +0.006 by dropping [M+Na]+. Either split the GLACIER features by adduct (gl_mh, gl_na, plus max/mean over spectra) so LambdaRank can weight them, or ablate [M+Na]+.
- (b) A/B the 2026-10-20 Zenodo glacier_msg.ckpt (membership-aware intensity attention, crossing-bond features) against the ms-pred MSG checkpoint on the isomer harness. Confirm that the host's 'MassSpecGym-trained GLACIER' ruling covers this newer release.
- (c) A/B instrument=QTOF vs Orbitrap for the timsTOF spectra (embed-instrument is on in the MSG config; the runner currently falls back to Orbitrap).
Inference only, CPU or T4. No TPU work needed.

### [forward-models] Contrastive fine-tuning of GLACIER on train.parquet, using our own pool isomers as decoys

- Source: GLACIER paper, Appendix E and Tables 4 and 11-15 (arXiv 2606.29161). ICEBERG 2.0: Wang et al., 'Neural Spectral Prediction for Structure Elucidation with Tandem Mass Spectrometry', bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.05.28.656653 (PMC12154671), Extended Data Table 1. Code: ms-pred src/ms_pred/glacier/train_contr_joint.py and configs/glacier/joint_contr_finetune_msg.yaml (MIT).
- What: Start from the pretrained GLACIER. For each training spectrum, predict spectra for the true structure plus k decoys: 50% same-formula PubChem structures and 50% one-step graph mutations (the MSG config uses 5 sampled + 5 fixed decoys from a pool of 20). The loss is a Sinkhorn soft-rank on entropy distances, ReLU(-log(d1 + delta)), added to the intensity loss. The fragment and MAGMa losses are dropped in this stage, so only spectra and a decoy map are needed. The decoy map (pubchem_formulae_inchikey.hdf5) can be swapped for our own pool.
- Evidence: GLACIER with vs without CF:
- NIST20 random split top-1 0.390 -> 0.525 (delta 0.4, d 5).
- NIST20 scaffold split 0.384 -> 0.460.
- MSG formula 48.5 -> 49.9, mass 69.7 -> 70.0.
Cosine falls 0.821 -> 0.802, so CF trades spectral fidelity for discrimination. Harsher settings help in-distribution splits most. Filtering retrieval decoys out of CF gives the same result, so the gain is not leakage. ICEBERG 2.0 gained only +0.006 top-1 from CF (0.288 -> 0.294) and needed 28.1 GPU-hours to run ICEBERG-Generate on the decoys; GLACIER avoids that cost because it generates fragments on GPU. The model is small (hidden 256, 5 Graphormer layers, ~0.05 GB checkpoint); the MSG fine-tune config budgets 1.5 GPU-days.
- Fit: Targets the COCONUT gap directly. Same-formula COCONUT isomers account for 58-68% of our lost class-2 MRR, and the decoys can be exactly those structures (COCONUT-not-train + PubChemLite + ChEBI/LMSD in the formula group). Fine-tuning on Enveda timsTOF spectra (including the Enamine library) also adapts intensities and collision energy to the test instrument, which MSG (Orbitrap/QTOF) lacks.
Eligibility: allowed MSG weights + train.parquet + COCONUT -> OK; the fine-tune code must be published. Hold out HL-val structures and the stage-2 OOF folds, and refit stage 2 on the fine-tuned scores.
Compute: T4, not TPU (Graphormer with dynamic fragment counts and DGL-style ops is a poor fit for XLA). Run 1-2 epochs over ~150-300k selected spectra within the 10 h/week. The dataset class may still read MAGMa outputs, which can be generated with ms_pred.magma on Kaggle CPU kernels.
Risks: over-fitting to library structures (class-1 regression; we already see -0.014), and gains on novel scaffolds will be smaller than on the random split. Expect a few points of within-formula top-1, i.e. ~+0.005 to +0.015 LB.

### [forward-models] Forward-simulated spectra of the candidate pool as training data for the inverse model (PeakFormer)

- Source: 'MassSpecGym in the Wild', arXiv 2606.19624, Table 1 and App. A.2. github.com/coleygroup/MIST (MIT) with Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.23303289 (mist_msg_aug.ckpt; msg_synthetic.tar.gz, 12.1 GB, 78.45M GLACIER spectra for the 'refined-4M' MSG molecules; canopus 40M). github.com/coleygroup/FRIGID README (revised results).
- What: Run a forward model over many unlabeled structures and mix the simulated spectra into the training of the spectrum-to-fingerprint encoder. The MIST-aug recipe uses 75 real + 2,048 synthetic spectra per rank per step over a 50-synthetic-epoch budget; CANOPUS synthetic data is 2 adducts x 2 instruments x 5 collision energies (10-50 eV) per molecule. The inverse model thereby distils the forward model's structure knowledge and sees positives for structures that have no measured spectra.
- Evidence: MIST on MSG formula-bonus retrieval (Wild Table 1):
- No augmentation: fingerprint Tanimoto 0.262, R@1 9.57%.
- + ICEBERG (train split) spectra of PubChem molecules, test molecules excluded: 0.581, 53.76%.
- + the test molecules spiked into the simulated set, still with a train-only ICEBERG: 0.617, 63.85%.
- ICEBERG trained on test data: 0.640, 67.75%.
For comparison, ICEBERG used directly as a forward-retrieval scorer gets 36.18%. FRIGID's README later disclosed that its PubChem augmentation set had been chosen to resemble test formulae. With a clean 4M set, FRIGID-base MSG top-1 fell 16.09% -> 8.21%. So most of the gain comes from simulating molecules close to the queries.
Ours: GLACIER's population bias is only +0.019 excess win rate, vs +0.137 for PeakFormer's pf_bern, so it is a nearly unbiased teacher for COCONUT-not-train structures.
- Fit: Probably the largest forward-model lever, and it targets the COCONUT gap / PeakFormer population bias. In this competition the candidate pool is fixed and public, so simulating every pool structure is the legitimate analogue of the 63.85% setting; no test spectra are used. It directly counters the documented failure that COCONUT-not-train structures are never positives (PeakFormer within-formula MRR 0.38-0.49 vs public f.z 0.639).
Plan:
1. Run GLACIER-MSG on ~450k non-train pool structures: [M+H]+ and [M+Na]+, 2-3 collision energies, QTOF -> ~2-3M spectra (~5 parallel 4-core CPU kernels for ~4-6 h, or T4 batches).
2. Train PeakFormer on TPU v3-8 with mixed real/synthetic batches, a source token for synthetic spectra (set to real at inference), and the same-mass window negatives.
3. Judge on HL-val within-formula COCONUT MRR, then refit stage 2. GLACIER evidence will then be partly double-counted, so refit the stage-2 weights out of fold.
Eligibility: MSG GLACIER and COCONUT are permitted, so the derived data is reproducible.
Cheaper first step: the Zenodo msg_synthetic cache (formula-annotated peaks, from which exact m/z follows). It covers MSG's 4M molecules, not our pool, so check the overlap first.

### [forward-models] Predicted spectra as library entries: an in-silico library for the whole pool

- Source: NEIMS: Wei et al., ACS Cent. Sci. 2019 (arXiv 1811.08545; github.com/brain-research/deep-molecular-massspec, Apache-2.0). FastEI: Nat. Commun. 2023 (s41467-023-39279-7). CFM-ID 4.0 web server: NAR 2022 (PMC9252813, Table 1). FIORA: Nat. Commun. 2025 (motivation). Ours: docs/plan.md ('deeper shortlist before GLACIER ~+0.001'; 'full-library matching hurt').
- What: Predict spectra once for every candidate structure and store them as pseudo-library entries. A query is then matched against the real library and the predicted library together, or the predicted similarity is used as a feature for every candidate in the mass window rather than only the top 30.
- Evidence: - NEIMS (EI) library augmentation, NIST17 queries: R@1 54.3%, R@10 92.7% with predicted spectra, vs 77% R@1 for the real NIST14 library and 42.6% for CFM-EI. Prediction takes about 5 ms per molecule.
- FastEI: 50% R@1 for compounds outside NIST17 using a million-scale NEIMS library.
- CFM-ID 4.0 in-silico library search ([M+H]+): top-1 48.1% against HMDB 5.0 (~250k compounds) but only 7.0% against a ~2.1M PubChem subset. Accuracy collapses as the library grows.
- Ours: extending GLACIER beyond the stage-1 top 30 gains about +0.001; adding real full-library matching hurt on the LB.
- Fit: Low gain as a standalone channel. Predicted spectra are poorly calibrated against real library entries, and the gain shrinks as the library grows, which in our pool is mostly because of same-formula crowding. Use predicted similarity only within formula groups (z, rank, gap) and keep it separate from the real-library features.
Main value is as a by-product of the previous item (the same ~2-3M simulated spectra). It would lift GLACIER coverage and depth for all candidates, including the 29/400 molecules without a covered spectrum once a negative-mode model exists, and give the PubChem proposal channel a second score for its top PeakFormer proposals.
Running GLACIER over the full 15.8M-compound PubChem tier is too costly (~25+ CPU-days at 8.6/s/thread), so restrict it to the top ~50 proposals per query.

### [forward-models] MARASON: forward model augmented with retrieved reference spectra (neural graph matching)

- Source: Wang et al., 'Neural Graph Matching Improves Retrieval Augmented Generation in Molecular Machine Learning', ICML 2025 (PMLR v267; arXiv 2502.17874). Code: ms-pred src/ms_pred/marason and configs/marason/*_msg.yaml (MIT).
- What: For a target structure, retrieve the most Tanimoto-similar training structure and up to 3 of its reference spectra at the closest collision energies. Both structures are fragmented with ICEBERG-Generate. - Evidence: NIST20 random split top-1: MARASON 0.278, ICEBERG 0.189, ICEBERG with collision energy 0.202, MassFormer 0.191, SCARF 0.187, NEIMS 0.105 (FFN) / 0.175 (GNN), 3DMolMS 0.055. Scaffold split: 0.283 vs ICEBERG 0.206. [M+H]+: 0.331 vs 0.270.
MSG formula R@1 34.03%: above FraGNNet (31.93) but below ICEBERG 2.0 (44.35) and GLACIER (49.88), so the base model matters more than retrieval augmentation.
Cosine by target-reference Tanimoto: retrieval helps from 0.5 up (0.8-0.9: 0.805 vs 0.759; 0.9-1.0: 0.824 vs 0.784) and is neutral or slightly harmful below 0.2 (0.550 vs 0.566). Naively concatenating the reference spectrum: -0.3% cosine.
- Fit: Conceptually right for class 2: train.parquet is a 2.5M-spectrum reference library, and the truth's best analog has median Tanimoto ~0.59 (>= 0.7 for 38-40% of queries).
In practice it is weak for us. The released MARASON is ICEBERG-1-based (CPU fragment enumeration, slow). MSG-trained MARASON weights are not on the host's allowed list, so we would have to retrain it (multi-GPU-days). Our analog channel already transfers analog spectra via Tanimoto, and our plan notes analog and GLACIER as complementary (0.870 fused).
Cheap approximation to test instead: a 'residual' feature, sim(query, GLACIER(cand) + [real(analog) - GLACIER(analog)]), aligned by fragment m/z shifts. Limit it to analogs with Tanimoto >= 0.5. It costs only extra GLACIER calls on the analogs.

### [forward-models] Fusion recipes: how forward scores are combined with inverse retrieval, with measured effects

- Source: Ours: docs/plan.md (Oct 18-24 sections). Public: kaggle.com/code/alexchilton/casmi26-public-lineage-0-34-to-0-44; kaggle.com/code/dariushafshar/casmi26-the-public-chain-version-by-version (board noise); the fusion_core.py embedded in kaggle.com/code/lehau007/casmi26-sota-v32-fiora-tri-forward-0420. Literature: Blazenovic et al., J. Cheminf. 2017 (CASMI 2016 combination); FRIGID hallucination scoring (arXiv 2604.16648).
- What: Four recipes have been tried:
1. Learned reranker (ours): LambdaRank over stage-1 features plus forward features computed within the formula group (z-score, rank, gap to the best other member), trained on out-of-fold stage-1 scores.
2. Linear z-fusion (public): inside each formula group, z(ranker) + lambda_ICE * z(ICE) + lambda_GL * z(GL), with forward weights set to 0 on library hits.
3. Slot-keeping swaps: forward scores only reorder candidates within fixed positions.
4. Rank fusion (RRF) across engines.
- Evidence: - Ours: learned stage 2 scores 0.89 OOF vs 0.71 for slot-keeping within-formula GLACIER swaps (weighted class 2: 0.68 vs 0.59). Stage 2 adds +0.010 LB.
- Ours, complementarity: FP 0.568 + GLACIER 0.743 -> 0.804 fused; with analog + ICEBERG -> 0.870. GLACIER: COCONUT +0.033 to +0.040, class 1 -0.014.
- Public: library-hit shield 0.404 -> 0.404; union rescoring +0.004; GLACIER [M+H]+ only +0.004 / +0.006. Locking rank 1: offline 0.63 -> 0.89 but board 0.413 -> 0.409.
- Blazenovic 2017 (CASMI 2016): library-only 60% top-1 -> 87% (challenge) / 93% (training) when combining MAGMa+, CFM-ID and compound-importance priors.
- Board resolution: ~132 public molecules; one extra rank-1 hit = 0.0076.
- Fit: Keep the learned stage 2. Cheap additions:
- Per-adduct and per-collision-energy GLACIER features, plus max vs mean over a molecule's spectra.
- Several similarity views: entropy, cosine on sqrt intensities, the share of query intensity explained by predicted fragments, and FRIGID-style 'hallucinated' predicted intensity absent from the query.
- Interaction features with lib_max (so stage 2 can down-weight GLACIER for class 1 itself instead of a hard shield) and LightGBM monotone constraints on the GLACIER z-features.
All of these sit at board-noise scale (<= one molecule each), so decide them on HL-val and the weighted class-2 CV, not on single LB reads.

### [forward-models] FRIGID: forward-model-guided refinement of de novo candidates (class 3)

- Source: Bohde et al., 'FRIGID: Scaling Diffusion-Based Molecular Generation from Mass Spectra at Training and Inference Time', ICML 2026 (PMLR v306; arXiv 2604.16648). github.com/coleygroup/FRIGID (revised-results README; ICEBERG MSG/CANOPUS weights bundled). Host-allowed.
- What: A MIST fingerprint and the formula condition a masked-diffusion language model that generates SAFE strings. ICEBERG then simulates spectra for the generated molecules. Predicted peaks absent from the experimental spectrum mark 'hallucinated' atoms, which are re-masked and re-denoised; the loop repeats. Accuracy scales log-linearly with the number of refinement rounds.
- Evidence: Revised results (clean MIST 4M-aug encoders), top-1 with known formula:
- MSG: FRIGID-base 8.21% -> 9.05% after 10 ICEBERG-guided rounds; top-10 9.82% -> 12.23%.
- NPLIB1: 13.31% -> 14.16%; top-10 20.15% -> 25.27%.
The earlier, leaky numbers were 18.29% and 25.03%. Decoder pretraining scales from 9.22% to 20.92% top-1 between 3.2M and 230M training structures (500 GPU-hours).
Ours: GLACIER rescoring of novel candidates raises proxy class-3 MRR 0.624 -> 0.725, but only 15.8% of proxy queries are reachable; the novel-only LB probe scores 0.010.
- Fit: Class 3 is 39-52% of the test set, but the realistic ceiling is low: ~9% top-1 on MSG with an oracle formula, and slow DLM sampling on a T4 within the 9 h budget. Best case is ~+0.01 to +0.02 LB, and the eligibility of every bundled checkpoint (MIST encoder, DLM) must be checked.
The transferable part is cheap: use GLACIER (8x faster than ICEBERG) to score generated or derived class-3 candidates within their formula group, as we already do, and add the 'hallucinated predicted intensity' penalty as a feature.
Only worth doing if a FRIGID inference kernel fits on the T4 after items 2-3.

### [foundation] DreaMS self-supervised backbone (ssl_model.ckpt), fine-tuned by us on train.parquet

- Source: Bushuiev et al., Nat. Biotechnol. 44:630-640 (online May 2025), https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-025-02663-3 ; code https://github.com/pluskal-lab/DreaMS (MIT); weights Zenodo 10997887 (ssl_model.ckpt 1.39 GB, embedding_model.ckpt 1.24 GB, CC-BY-4.0); GeMS on HF roman-bushuiev/GeMS (MIT; GeMS_A10.hdf5 14.6 GB); fine-tuning tutorial https://dreams-docs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/fine_tuning.html ; host rulings in Kaggle threads 749217 and 748882
- What: A 116M-parameter BERT-style transformer (7 layers, d=1024, 8 heads, pre-norm, no biases) that reads the top 60 peaks during pretraining and the top 100 during fine-tuning. Each m/z goes through fixed multi-frequency sin/cos Fourier features and a 5-layer FFN, and a Graphormer-style attention term attends to pairwise m/z differences. A precursor token serves as the spectrum embedding. Pretraining loss is 0.8 x masked-m/z classification (30% of peaks masked, sampled by intensity, 20,000 bins over 0-1000 Da) plus 0.2 x retention-order prediction for two spectra from the same LC-MS run. 20% of spectra get a random m/z shift of 0-50. Fine-tuning swaps the head and trains end to end, with early stopping on a Murcko-histogram split.
- Evidence: Pretraining: GeMS-A10 (24M LSH-clustered MassIVE spectra, 97% Orbitrap), 48 h on 8xA100 or 4xMI250X. Data quality beat size: 24M A10 spectra outperformed 100M-spectrum GeMS-A1000/GeMS-B. Fine-tuning took no more than several hours. The tutorial job uses lr 3e-5, batch 64/GPU x 8 GPUs, 100 peaks, --train_precision 64, a 10 h wall limit and max_epochs 103. Embedding speed is about 1M spectra/h on one A100. Fine-tuned 4096-bit Morgan head: on par with MIST on MIST-CANOPUS retrieval (no gain claimed). Contrastive fine-tune (triplet loss, ~25k MoNA spectra) beats MS2DeepScore and 44 classical similarities on 10-ppm same-mass retrieval. Independent checks: (a) LSM-MS2 paper, MassSpecGym library search top-1 per spectrum: cosine 0.725 vs DreaMS embedding 0.726. (b) MSAlign Table 4 (MSG formula split, frozen encoders, R@1): binned->Morgan 34.3 vs DreaMS->Morgan 38.6 (+4.3); binned->MolDeBERTa 44.5 vs DreaMS->MolDeBERTa 47.5 (+3.0). (c) Audit arXiv 2606.19624: DreaMS (last 2 layers trainable) + adapter to ChemBERTa with InfoNCE gives 6.70% hit@1 on formula-bonus MSG after the canonicalization fix, below MIST 9.57, MVP 13.96 and FLARE 22.66. No published pretrained-vs-scratch ablation exists for MSG structure retrieval.
- Fit: Small expected gain for class 2 and the analog channel. PeakFormer already uses exact-m/z sinusoids plus neutral losses, and our labelled set (2.5M spectra / 275k structures) is about 10x MassSpecGym, which shrinks the benefit of pretraining (my estimate: +0.002 to +0.01 MRR at best). Cheap tests: (1) feed the frozen 1024-d DreaMS-SSL precursor embedding into PeakFormer or into an aligner (see MSAlign item); (2) initialise a transformer from ssl_model.ckpt, fine-tune with our 15k-bit FP + same-mass-window contrastive heads, and A/B it against scratch on HL-val. Domain gap: GeMS-A is Orbitrap but the test set is timsTOF. Option: 1-2 epochs of continued masked-m/z pretraining on train.parquet timsTOF spectra (no labels needed). Test-time self-supervision on the hidden spectra is an open rules question (forum thread 752297), so confirm before trying it. TPU v3-8: fixed 100-peak padding gives static shapes, which suits XLA. DreaMS trains in fp64 for m/z precision and TPUs lack fast fp64, so compute the Fourier features in float64 on the host data loader and feed fp32/bf16. Rough cost (my estimate): ~70 GFLOP per spectrum per train step, ~1.8e17 FLOP per 2.5M-spectrum epoch, so ~0.5-1.5 h/epoch on v3-8 at 25-35% MFU and 3+ h/epoch on T4. Use the TPU. Eligibility: the host confirmed ssl_model.ckpt is allowed (749217: 'Yes, all of those are allowed'). Ship the checkpoint, a reference to the authors' code and GeMS, and our own fine-tuning code (748882 A). Third-party DreaMS fine-tunes without a published recipe are NOT allowed. The authors' embedding_model.ckpt was fine-tuned on MoNA but validated/evaluated on NIST20. Our existing DreaMS analog channel may use it, so confirm it with the host or re-derive our own embedding head from ssl_model.ckpt.

### [foundation] MSAlign recipe: frozen foundation encoders + small aligners trained with candidate-list InfoNCE + late score fusion

- Source: Krzakala et al., 'MSAlign: Aligning Molecule and Mass Spectra Foundation Models for Metabolite Identification', arXiv 2605.19752 (May 2026, CC BY 4.0), code https://github.com/KrzakalaPaul/MSAlign-NeurIPS2026 ; supporting loss study: 'Small molecule retrieval from tandem mass spectrometry: what are we optimizing for?', arXiv 2602.16507
- What: Precompute frozen spectrum embeddings (DreaMS or a 0.1 Da binned vector) and frozen molecule embeddings (Morgan FP or a molecular foundation model). Train two MLP towers (LayerNorm/GELU) into a shared space with 'candidate InfoNCE': a softmax over the query's own same-mass/formula candidate list instead of in-batch negatives. Several aligner variants plus Gaussian mass-agreement scores are then late-fused with weights fitted on validation. All SMILES are RDKit-canonicalised.
- Evidence: MSG formula split, R@1: candidate InfoNCE 47.5 vs in-batch InfoNCE 19.5 vs regression ms->mol 16.7. Molecule side: Morgan->MolDeBERTa +10.2; graph FM MHG-GED 47.0, ChemBERTa-13M 46.9, MoLFormer 46.5 (all ~equal). Spectrum side: DreaMS vs binned +4.3. Fusion: 47.5 -> 58.2 (+10.7, near-additive: aligners +6.9, mass scorers +3.8). Official MSG MCES split, R@1: MSAlign 19.0, fused 30.8, vs their re-runs of MIST 16.4, MVP 25.6, FLARE 28.4 (these need the formula). Training takes 0.82 h on one V100 (fused set 3.3 h) plus ~5 h of one-off embedding precompute. Loss study (2602.16507, MSG formula candidates, HR@1): listwise contrastive 13.62 vs BCE 10.08 vs IoU 6.48. Retrieval-aligned losses win at the cost of FP Tanimoto.
- Fit: A cheap, eligible new stage-1 feature family, aimed at class 2 (COCONUT/PubChem). Build 2-4 aligners {DreaMS-SSL embedding, our binned input} x {Morgan/our 15k FP, frozen molecular FM}. Train them on train.parquet using the same-mass windows we already build; PeakFormer's window negatives are the key ingredient and are already in place. Feed the scores to LambdaRank, which already does the fusion. Molecule-FM embeddings for the whole pool (~1-2M structures x 768 fp16, roughly 1.5-3 GB) are precomputed offline on T4 or TPU; the notebook only does lookups plus MLPs. The incremental value over our FP dual encoder is mainly the molecule-FM side. Licences: the hosts allow chemical language/graph models (thread 751373). MolDeBERTa is CC-BY-NC-ND-4.0, so avoid it. Use ibm/materials.mhg-ged (Apache-2.0, graph-based) or Derify/ChemBERTa_augmented_pubchem_13m (Apache-2.0, PubChem). Concrete risk: our simulated class-2 training queries have train.parquet-style SMILES, while real class-2 answers come from COCONUT/PubChemLite. A SMILES-LM tower can learn 'train-formatted SMILES = answer', the same shortcut that inflated a DreaMS+ChemBERTa aligner from 6.7% to 82.4% in the audit. Regenerate every candidate SMILES with one RDKit pipeline (stereo stripped; the metric is InChIKey14) or prefer the graph FM.

### [foundation] Synthetic-spectrum pretraining: distil GLACIER/ICEBERG into the inverse model over the whole candidate pool (FRIGID / MIST-aug recipe)

- Source: 'MassSpecGym in the Wild' (Liu, Bushuiev, Lightheart, ..., Healey, Pluskal, Coley), arXiv 2606.19624, Table 1 and App. A.2; FRIGID, Bohde et al., arXiv 2604.16648; coleygroup MIST repo https://github.com/coleygroup/MIST + Zenodo 23303289 (MIT, 2026-10-20)

Validation uses real held-out spectra only.
- Evidence: MSG formula-bonus retrieval hit@1: original MIST 9.57%. The 435k pool may overlap MSG decoys (unverified). Ready-made assets in Zenodo 23303289: msg_synthetic.tar.gz (78,450,500 GLACIER spectra from a 'refined-4M' molecule set, 12.08 GB, stored as MIST compact features = top-10 formula-annotated fragments + precursor token); canopus_synthetic (40M spectra = 2M molecules x {[M+H]+,[M+Na]+} x {Orbitrap,QTOF} x 5 CEs 10-50 eV); mist_msg_aug.ckpt; glacier_msg.ckpt.
- Fit: Highest-upside item in this sweep, aimed at class 2 / COCONUT (our COCONUT-not-train share 0.208 vs public lineage 0.252). Simulating spectra for EVERY structure in our candidate pool (COCONUT + PubChemLite + ChEBI/LIPID MAPS) uses the pool transductively, not the test labels, so it is legitimate. It is the analogue of 'Setting 2', worth +10 pts over Setting 3 on MSG. This moves GLACIER knowledge from stage 2 (top-30 only) into stage 1, where recall@30 is decided. Also simulate the held-out train structures used as simulated class-2 queries, so 'has a synthetic spectrum' cannot become a shortcut. Bottleneck: GLACIER throughput (DGL, GPU). Measure it from our stage-2 kernel. At ~20 sims/s on T4, 1.2M structures x 2 CEs is ~33 T4-hours, about 3 weeks of our T4 quota. So prioritise COCONUT-not-train structures in the observed precursor range, spread work over CPU kernels, or check whether the msg_synthetic cache stores SMILES/InChIKeys and overlaps COCONUT (unknown). Without identifiers it is only generic FP pretraining data. Eligibility: hosts approved the GLACIER/ICEBERG MSG checkpoints and the official FRIGID checkpoints. The MIST Zenodo record was asked about on the forum (751373) with no host answer visible, and the provenance of the refined-4M list must be permitted data. Safest is to generate our own corpus with the approved GLACIER checkpoint and public code. If any MIST checkpoint is used, it needs formula annotation and has a known batched-inference padding-mask bug (audit section 6.1).

### [foundation] Benchmark-hygiene calibration: MassSpecGym audit + SpecBridge withdrawal (which foundation-model retrieval gains are real)

- Source: arXiv 2606.19624 (MIT / IOCB / Enveda / Tufts, June 2026; MassSpecGym v1.5 at https://github.com/pluskal-lab/MassSpecGym); SpecBridge arXiv 2601.17204, withdrawn 3 Mar 2026 with the note 'We have found a problem in the preprocessing/evaluation pipeline'
- What: An audit of 26 papers reporting MassSpecGym results in its first year. It sorts failures into three groups: leakage through pretraining data or shared components (formula annotators, simulators), shortcut learning from candidate-set artifacts (SMILES canonicalization, PubChem ranking order), and implementation/metric bugs. SpecBridge (DreaMS fine-tuned into frozen ChemBERTa space) had claimed 84.7% R@1 before withdrawal.
- Fit: Not a model but a filter on what to try in 7 weeks. (1) Discount any foundation-model retrieval claim that uses a SMILES-LM candidate encoder unless canonicalization is uniform. Per the corrected leaderboard, SSL spectrum encoders alone sit at 6-14% while simulation-augmented supervised encoders reach 54%. (2) Apply the same hygiene to our pool: canonicalize all sources identically before any SMILES-derived feature, so source formatting cannot leak through our simulated class-2 queries. (3) Spectrum-blind popularity priors are extremely strong on PubChem-derived benchmarks. That matches our documentation priors, but the hidden Enveda natural-product test set need not share the prior, so keep validating on HL-val rather than MSG. (4) MSG v1.5 offers 'data-safe' retrains of DreaMS, ICEBERG and MIST-CF. They are useful only for MSG-based sanity checks. No compute cost.

### [denovo] FRIGID: masked-diffusion SAFE decoder conditioned on a predicted fingerprint and formula, with forward-model-guided remasking (MIT Coley lab, ICML 2026)

- Source: arXiv 2604.16648v2 (PMLR 306, ICML 2026); https://github.com/coleygroup/FRIGID (README results revised Oct 21 2026; Zenodo records 23543494 and 23303289)
- What: A 247M-parameter masked diffusion language model (built on NVIDIA GenMol) generates SAFE strings. It conditions on two inputs through cross-attention: the set of active bits of a predicted 4096-bit radius-2 Morgan fingerprint (from a MIST encoder) and per-element formula counts. It is pretrained without spectra on up to 1B SAFE-GPT structures, with benchmark test and validation InChIKeys removed. Refinement then scores candidates with ICEBERG, maps simulated peaks that are absent from the query back to SAFE tokens, remasks those tokens and re-denoises them (B=128 samples, K=12 refined candidates, M=8 variants, up to 25 rounds). Final candidates are ranked by fingerprint Tanimoto to the predicted fingerprint, with formula-matched candidates first.
- Evidence: All numbers assume a known formula. The README revision of Oct 21 2026 halves the arXiv numbers, because the earlier MIST encoders were 'trained on PubChem molecules selected using test-set formulae'. Revised top-1 / top-10: NPLIB1 FRIGID-base 13.31% / 20.15%, FRIGID with 10 rounds 14.16% / 25.27%. MassSpecGym FRIGID-base 8.21% / 9.82%, FRIGID 9.05% / 12.23%. The superseded arXiv v2 values were NPLIB1 25.03% / 33.37% and MassSpecGym 18.29% / 22.00%. Formula unknown (arXiv, top-5 MIST-CF formulas, same budget): MassSpecGym 18.29 -> 14.28 top-1 (base 16.09 -> 11.60) and NPLIB1 25.03 -> 21.79, a relative loss of about 13-28%. Accuracy depends steeply on fingerprint quality (arXiv Table 9, MassSpecGym top-1 by Tanimoto of predicted to true FP): 0.2% below 0.25, 3.0% in 0.25-0.5, 7.7% in 0.5-0.75, 42.4% at 0.75 or above. The NPLIB1 equivalents are 0 / 6.1 / 23.6 / 60.0%. Speed on an RTX 6000 Ada: base 6.58 s per spectrum (20x faster than DiffMS at 131 s); 10 rounds 41.3 s (README) or 86.5 s (paper). Fine-tuning on benchmark spectra did not help (Table 10).
- Fit: This is the best class-3 generator we can use, and the hosts explicitly allow FRIGID (Apache-2.0 code). The integration is simple because PeakFormer's first block is already Morgan r2 4096 (casmi/richfp.py), the same definition FRIGID conditions on. We can therefore drop the MassSpecGym-trained MIST, which is out of domain for timsTOF, and feed PeakFormer's morgan2 probabilities straight into the released decoder with no decoder retraining. Formula hypotheses come from our mass-window logic; send the top 2-3 formulas. Use the MassSpecGym ICEBERG (allowed) for refinement, or GLACIER if its peak-to-fragment attribution is exposed. Run it only on queries gated as likely class 3 (low stage-1 confidence) and merge the outputs as a candidate source into stage 2, not as a raw list. Compute, assuming a T4 is 3-4x slower than an Ada: about 25 s per query for base sampling, so 150 gated queries take about 1 h on one T4. Ten refinement rounds would take about 6 h, so cap it at 3 rounds or about 60 queries. This needs a GPU in the scoring notebook; on CPU, benchmark first. Cheap go/no-go test: measure the Tanimoto distribution of PeakFormer morgan2 predictions on the PubChem-absent training proxy and the Zenodo Enveda-absent set, then apply the Table 9 bins. Example: 30/40/25/5% across the four bins gives about 5% top-1. My realistic expectation for class 3 is 3-8% top-1 and 8-15% within the top 25.

### [denovo] Multi-threshold fingerprint querying with generation-frequency consensus (MS-GPT), plus warnings about inflated 2026 claims (MS-GPT, DualLGD, MolForge)

- Source: MS-GPT arXiv 2607.23607 (Jul 2026, https://github.com/VIKI623/MS-GPT); DualLGD arXiv 2605.07048 (May 2026); FRIGID arXiv 2604.16648 Appendix A.3 (MIST batching bug)
- What: MS-GPT adds formula and fingerprint conditioning to SAFE-GPT (150M parameters, pretrained 2 epochs on about 100M molecules). Instead of thresholding the predicted fingerprint posterior once, it builds about 20 queries across a threshold band whose active-bit density is calibrated to match the training fingerprints. It draws 5 samples per query (B=100), then ranks InChIKey14 groups by how often they were generated, plus a LoRA adapter on posterior-derived queries. DualLGD is a DiffMS-style graph diffusion model with separate atom and bond (line-graph) streams.
- Evidence: MS-GPT claims top-1 / top-10 of 29.76% / 41.07% on NPLIB1 and 23.91% / 28.65% on MassSpecGym. However, its Appendix B.5 says it used 'the dataset-specific MIST checkpoint released by FRIGID', in July, before FRIGID revised those encoders as leaky on Oct 21 2026. FRIGID's own MassSpecGym top-1 fell from 18.3% to 9.1% after that revision, so MS-GPT is probably inflated by a similar factor. The ablation is still informative. Band querying versus the best single threshold: NPLIB1 28.42 / 33.78 against 24.40 / 28.87; MassSpecGym 22.77 / 26.96 against 20.08 / 22.80, a gain of about 3-5 points. The LoRA adapter adds +1.3 / +7.3 on NPLIB1. DualLGD claims 34.37% / 44.96% on NPLIB1 and 23.89% / 33.08% on MassSpecGym, and 25.53% on NPLIB1 with no pretraining at all, where DiffMS gets 0.50%. It reuses DiffMS-released MIST weights and has not been replicated. FRIGID Appendix A.3 shows that a padding-mask bug in batched MIST inference raised MolForge on MassSpecGym from 10.73% to 31.75% top-1 at batch size 24.
- Fit: Adopt only the cheap idea: when FRIGID is fed PeakFormer probabilities, sample over several thresholds (an active-bit density band calibrated on training structures) and pool by InChIKey14. The generation count becomes a stage-2 feature. This costs nothing extra and is worth about +3-5 points of top-1 in the source ablation. Do not port DualLGD or plan around 24-34% top-1 figures until someone outside the authors reproduces them. Every MassSpecGym number produced with old batched MIST code is suspect.

### [denovo] Scaffold- or analog-conditioned generation (MADGEN; SAFE fragment remasking seeded by our analogs)

- Source: MADGEN, ICLR 2025, arXiv 2501.01950; GenMol/SAFE fragment-constrained generation as used in FRIGID's refinement (arXiv 2604.16648 Sec. 4 and B.3)
- What: MADGEN has two stages. It first ranks candidate Murcko scaffolds for the spectrum with a contrastive retriever, then grows the full molecule from the scaffold with spectrum-attended graph diffusion. The same principle maps onto SAFE decoders: keep the fragments of a known analog that agree with the spectrum, mask the fragments implicated by shifted peaks, and let the formula- and fingerprint-conditioned decoder fill them in. FRIGID's refinement already does exactly this to its own samples.
- Evidence: MADGEN with predicted scaffolds: top-1 4.6% (NIST23), 2.10% (CANOPUS/NPLIB1), 1.31% (MassSpecGym). With an oracle scaffold: 49.0% top-1 and 65.5% top-10 (NIST23), 18.7% / 22.2% (CANOPUS), 10.5% / 12.4% (MassSpecGym). Scaffold retrieval accuracy is only 13.2-40.3%, and the gap between predicted and oracle scaffolds is 10-20x. Accuracy falls as the number of free atoms outside the scaffold grows. Speed is 23.8 s per spectrum (2.5M parameters).
- Fit: Scaffold quality is the whole game, and we already have scaffold sources: the analog channel (best analog Tanimoto to the truth about 0.59, at least 0.7 in 37-40% of cases) and modification-site localisation (casmi/siteloc.py, which raised derivative MRR from 0.061 to 0.107). Do not train MADGEN. Instead, seed FRIGID refinement with our top analogs: convert them to SAFE, mask the fragments that carry shifted-peak sites, and re-denoise under PeakFormer's fingerprint and the target formula. This is a generalised version of the transform table in casmi/derive.py, which covers 24% of PubChem-absent proxies, and it is not limited to hand-written rules. It is the most promising class-3 lever for derivatives of known natural products. Expected effect: it extends derivative reach beyond the current 15.8% of reachable proxy queries.

### [denovo] FOAM: formula-constrained graph genetic algorithm with a forward-model objective, seeded by database or generator structures

- Source: Manjrekar, Wang, Goldman, Fromer, Coley, arXiv 2602.07709 (Feb 2026)
- What: Seeds are formula-matched structures from PubChem plus optional DiffMS samples. A Jensen-style graph GA (as implemented in PMO) applies crossover and mutation that preserve the formula. Every offspring is scored by the spectral similarity between its ICEBERG-predicted spectrum and the query. The search stops after 1,500-7,500 oracle calls per spectrum, and the candidate list is the final population ranked by predicted-spectrum similarity.
- Evidence: NIST'20 random split (NIST-trained ICEBERG, so for reference only), with the true structure excluded from the PubChem seeds: after 3 generations the truth is in the top 10 31% of the time and at top 1 11% of the time. Mean best Tanimoto in the top 10 rises from 0.52 for the seeds to 0.62. Results depend on seed similarity: top-10 exact is 8.1% when the closest seed has Tanimoto 0.2-0.4 and 31.5% at 0.4-0.6. They depend on the oracle too: 53.4% when ICEBERG's predicted spectrum of the truth has cosine of at least 0.8. Ranking quality peaks after 2-3 generations and then declines as the GA exploits oracle errors and finds decoys. MassSpecGym (ICEBERG retrained on its training split, DiffMS plus PubChem seeds, 1 generation): top-1 1.50% (DiffMS alone 2.30%), top-10 10.28% (DiffMS alone 4.25%), Tanimoto at top 10 0.53. The truth is encountered in 33% of searches, against 5.8% inside the DiffMS seeds.
- Fit: This fits directly with what we already have: seeds from our stage-1 top 30, the analog channel and NovelEngine derivatives (most class-3 truths have a seed at Tanimoto 0.6 or above, which FOAM's bins treat as the good regime), and GLACIER as the eligible oracle (MassSpecGym-trained, 8.6 predictions per second per thread on CPU). To limit decoy exploitation, blend GLACIER with the PeakFormer f.z score and stop after 2-3 generations. Budget: 1,500 calls on each of 150 gated queries is about 225k GLACIER calls, roughly 2 h on 4 CPU threads, and needs no GPU. Feed the final population into stage 2 as a candidate source. Expected on class 3: 5-12% within the top 10, less at rank 1, because GLACIER on timsTOF is a weaker oracle than NIST-trained ICEBERG on NIST.

### [denovo] Formula-free and retrieval-augmented generation (MARLIN, SiTGen)

- Source: MARLIN arXiv 2607.04774 (Jul 2026, UNC Charlotte); SiTGen, Research Square preprint rs-10484307/v1
- What: MARLIN is a block-diffusion SAFE decoder warm-started from FRIGID. It conditions on a predicted fingerprint (from a DreaMS or MIST encoder) and the measured precursor mass only, never a formula. A provably safe heavy-atom pruning step keeps every candidate within 10 ppm of the measured mass, and it draws 384 samples per spectrum. SiTGen retrieves reference structures from an in-silico spectral library of 862,963 structures, reranks them, and conditions a transformer on the spectrum, the formula and those references.
- Evidence: MARLIN on NPLIB1 with no formula: 16.94% top-1 and 23.54% top-10 with the DreaMS encoder; 19.18% top-1 with MIST plus the MIST-CF top-1 formula. It recovers the right formula as a byproduct for 76.7% of spectra, against 32.6% for FRIGID in the same formula-free setting; MIST-CF alone reaches 82.3% top-1 formula accuracy. Accuracy at 500 Da or more is 11.4% (DreaMS) against 6.7% (FRIGID). Speed is 34.1 s per spectrum on an L40S. Fingerprint-noise augmentation during training matters (16.94 drops to 13.20 without it). SiTGen on MassSpecGym: 9.48% top-1 and 18.74% top-10 (preprint, not verified).
- Fit: Both address real gaps in our setting. Formula errors cost FRIGID 13-28% relative, and our class-3 queries have no confirmed formula. Training noise matters because PeakFormer posteriors will be noisier on timsTOF unknowns. A DreaMS encoder is host-allowed. Practical takeaways without adopting MARLIN wholesale: send FRIGID 2-3 formula hypotheses rather than one, and condition or seed it on retrieved references (our stage-1 top-k and analogs), which is the SiTGen idea and costs nothing extra. If MARLIN's code and weights are clean, it is a drop-in alternative to FRIGID for formula-ambiguous queries, especially heavier natural products.

3. Drop any structure whose metric key is in the train ∪ COCONUT pool, then keep the top 25.
4. The 200 best-documented structures in the window are always added to the shortlist, which is scored z(f·z) + 0.15·[ln(1+SID) + ln(1+PMID)].
5. Merge only when lib_max < 0.9. The proposals take slots 2/4/6/8/10 when rel > 600, otherwise slots 4/8/12/16/20 (later also 24).
6. The top proposal is PROMOTED to rank 1 when rel = f·z(top PubChem) - max f·z(pool window) > 150, or when S > 6 and pop ≥ 5.
- Evidence: - The PubChem-only list ranked by f·z alone scored 0.037 public.
- Removing PubChem-only candidates from the 0.464 pipeline gave 0.409 (-0.055).

- Lowering the promotion threshold rel > 250 to 150: +0.003 to +0.006 on 4 different parents.

- Channel depth 1k to 5k, together with simulator weights 0.5 to 1.0: 0.373 to 0.381.

### [ranking-ml] Rank all sources by calibrated probability, using a per-query source gate instead of slots and rules

- Source: SIRIUS 6 docs, 'Expansive search' (https://v6.docs.sirius-ms.io/methods-background/); Duehrkop et al. SIRIUS 4 preprint (https://bio.informatik.uni-jena.de/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/preprint_sirius4.pdf); Si & Callan 2003 ACM TOIS 21:457, SSL result merging (doi 10.1145/944012.944017), with an SSL vs CORI comparison in arXiv 2203.00350; Kaggle public lineage kernels guhongbin/casmi26-v502-pc465-cslot (gated PubChem merge and promotion) and sanpier/pubchem-neural025-lb-0-466 (weighted RRF)
- What: Each molecule has exactly one correct InChIKey14, so expected RR = sum_i p(c_i)/i. By the rearrangement inequality, sorting every candidate from every source by its calibrated marginal probability maximises expected MRR. Fixed slots, interleaving and promotion rules only approximate this. Split the probability as p(c|x) = P(class s(c) | x) * P(c | x, s). The class gate covers {train-library, COCONUT-not-train, PubChem/other-only, not-in-pool}. P(c|x,s) is a softmax over candidates within one source (item 5). Sum the mass of InChIKey14 duplicates across sources. Some channels lack features: PubChem-only candidates have no library, analog or doc features. Map their scores onto the main scale with an isotonic or linear fit on the candidates that both channels score (SSL-style merging from federated search).
- Evidence: SIRIUS 6 expansive search first searches the requested DBs, then also PubChem. It switches to the PubChem results only if the PubChem top hit has a COSMIC confidence at least 2x that of the requested-DB top hit, so it uses a confidence-ratio gate rather than fixed slots. Restricting the DB acts as a prior: CSI:FingerID top-1 on CASMI 2016 (+) was 74.0% in a 0.5M biomolecule DB vs 39.4% in PubChem. The authors later said that bio DB also contained MINEs. In this competition, the public lineage merges PubChem-only candidates only when lib_max < 0.9. They take slots 2,4,6,8,10 when the FP margin is > 600, else slots 4,8,12,16,20. The top PubChem candidate is promoted to rank 1 if S = z(f.z within the PubChem pass-2 set) + 0.25*pop > 6 with pop >= 5, or if its raw f.z margin over the best pool candidate is > 150. The author's ablation: 0.417 with promotion vs 0.374 without, both with ICEBERG broken. They read this as promotion worth >= +0.024 LB, estimated ~+0.037 on a working base. The 'other' source gives them 0.057 vs our 0.008. Rank-only fusion gains little here: the top public kernel's weighted RRF (k=3, w=0.15) moved 0.465 -> 0.466. Federated-search evidence: SSL regression merging beat CORI (MAP 0.0725 vs 0.0650, cooperative setting), and learned per-source mappings (random forest) beat both (arXiv 2203.00350).
- Fit: Replace the PubChem/Enamine slot insertion and the hard source rules with a multinomial gate: LightGBM multiclass or logistic regression on query-level features from item 2. Train it on simulated queries stratified into 4 classes. (1) Train-library: keep other spectra of the structure. (2) COCONUT: hold out a train structure that is in COCONUT. (3) PubChem-only: remove the structure from train and COCONUT but keep it in the PubChem tier. (4) Not-in-pool: remove it everywhere. Final score: log P(s|x) + log softmax_s(c). Global source weights tuned on LB probes cannot do what this does, because the decision is made per query. Targets the 'other' gap (0.049) and the COCONUT gap (0.044). It also says when to spend slots on class-3 generated candidates (high P(not-in-pool|x)). CPU only, minutes. Eligible: train.parquet + PubChem + COCONUT. Guardrail: the train-only (0.156) and COCONUT-only (0.208) source-restricted LB probes should not drop.

### [ranking-ml] COSMIC-style calibrated confidence for each source's top hit (E-value against same-mass decoys + margin + list size, Platt)

- Source: Hoffmann et al. 2022, Nat Biotechnol 40:411, 'High-confidence structural annotation of metabolites absent from spectral libraries' (PMC8926923); SIRIUS 6 docs on COSMIC exact vs approximate modes
- What: First calibrate the raw retrieval score of a query's top hit against that query's own decoy distribution. COSMIC used the PubChem candidates of the query as decoy proxies, fitted a log-normal mixture or KDE, and derived a P/E-value. Then a linear SVM with enforced monotone feature directions combines the calibrated score, raw score, top1-runner-up margin, number of candidates and spectrum-quality features. Lists with a single candidate get a separate model. Platt scaling turns the output into a probability that the hit is correct. Ranking scores are built to order candidates within one query, not to compare confidence across queries or sources. This step makes them comparable.
- Evidence: CASMI 2016 (127 standards, structure-disjoint evaluation): ROC AUC for telling correct from incorrect top hits was 0.40-0.55 for raw MetFrag, MAGMa+, CFM-ID and CSI:FingerID scores, vs 0.82 for COSMIC. COSMIC gave 57 correct hits at FDR < 10% in the biomolecule DB (123 queries). Average rank of correct annotations when hits are ordered by confidence improved from 56.5 to 33.9. The most important features were the margin to the runner-up and the number of candidates. Candidate count strongly changed confidence; mass did not. A score of 0.64 is roughly 10% FDR. The public lineage's z(f.z over the PubChem pass-2 set) gate is a hand-tuned version of the E-value step.
- Fit: Compute per source (train, COCONUT, PubChem tier, generated): an E-value of the best PeakFormer/FP score against ~1-5k same-mass PubChem candidates, the top1-top2 margin within the source, log(#candidates in the window), lib_max cosine, and the GLACIER similarity of the hit. Feed these to the gate in item 1 and to any promotion decision. Train on structure-disjoint simulated-query folds using LightGBM monotone_constraints or a monotone logistic model. CPU minutes. Scoring the decoys uses PeakFormer inference over PubChem mass windows: a few minutes on T4 or TPU for ~400 molecules, and it also fits inside the 9 h notebook. Eligible.

### [ranking-ml] Estimate the hidden test's class/source mix with EM label-shift adaptation (Saerens EM + bias-corrected calibration)

- Source: Saerens, Latinne & Decaestecker 2002, Neural Computation 14:21, 'Adjusting the outputs of a classifier to new a priori probabilities'; Alexandari, Kundaje & Shrikumar, ICML 2020 (arXiv 1901.06852; code github.com/kundajelab/labelshiftexperiments); simulation precedent: MS2Query, de Jonge et al. 2023 Nat Commun (PMC10060387)
- What: Train a calibrated query-level classifier q(s|x) on simulated queries with known class priors pi_sim. The classes can be the 4 classes of item 1, or source x answer-documentation bin. Then iterate on the unlabeled hidden test: q'(s|x) is proportional to q(s|x) * pi(s)/pi_sim(s), and pi(s) <- mean over test of q'(s|x). The converged pi estimates the test mix: 16/45/39 vs 28/20/52? It also re-weights each query's gate posterior. EM needs calibration with per-class bias terms (BCTS = temperature + bias, or vector scaling) fitted on held-out simulated queries.
- Evidence: EM + BCTS gave the lowest error in estimated shift weights. CIFAR-100, Dirichlet alpha=0.1, n=7000: MSE 0.110, vs 0.409 for BBSL-soft, 0.266 for RLLS-soft, and 1.80 for uncalibrated EM. At 35% recall its analogues had average Tanimoto 0.63, vs 0.45 for modified cosine.
- Fit: Runs inside the notebook on hidden-test features in milliseconds. Uses: (a) set the priors of item 1's gate from data instead of community guesses or LB probes. (b) Make s = source x doc-count bin of the answer, so EM estimates how documented the true answers are. That replaces the hand-set doc-shift weights (v5w) with an estimate. This doc shift depends on the label, so it cannot be fixed by covariate weighting (item 4). Label shift assumes p(x|s) is the same in simulation and test, which the instrument shift breaks. Eligible: unsupervised, deterministic use of test features. Confirm the rules allow test-time adaptation.

Weight whole queries, not documents: ranking losses are per query, and wrong document weights corrupt many pairs.
- Evidence: LETOR 3.0 with RankSVM, MAP without weighting -> with query weighting (query-comp): HP03->TD03 0.2508->0.2816; NP03->TD03 0.1936->0.2392; TD04->NP04 0.5427->0.6541; TD03->HP03 0.6986->0.7297. Document-instance weighting was often at or below no weighting (TD03->HP03 doc-pair 0.6588). Kretschmer et al. show MS/MS and other small-molecule training sets do not cover biomolecular space uniformly (MCES distance, NP-likeness), so models do worse on real biological samples than evaluations suggest. This fits our +0.005 LB from debiasing PeakFormer (instrument token removed).
- Fit: Query features: precursor m/z, adduct, CE, #peaks, spectral entropy, instrument, best library cosine, PeakFormer top score and margin per source, #candidates per source. Train the domain classifier on simulated stage-1 queries vs the ~1,500 hidden test spectra inside the notebook. Clip weights to e.g. [0.2, 5] and normalise. Retrain stage 1 and stage 2 with them: in LightGBM put the same weight on every row of a query; XGBoost takes one weight per group. Retraining takes minutes to tens of minutes of CPU. Report the adversarial AUC first: near 0.5 means no gain to expect. This corrects covariate shift (instrument, chemistry), which complements the label shift handled in item 3. Eligible if test-time training is permitted.

### [ranking-ml] MRR-aligned listwise objectives for stages 1 and 2 (softmax CE, XGBoost rank:map, LightGBM rank_xendcg)

- Source: Bruch, Wang, Bendersky & Najork, ICTIR 2019, 'An analysis of the softmax cross entropy loss for learning-to-rank with binary relevance' (doi 10.1145/3341981.3344221); Donmez, Svore & Burges, SIGIR 2009, 'On the local optimality of LambdaRank'; XGBoost learning-to-rank tutorial (https://xgboost.readthedocs.io/en/stable/tutorials/learning_to_rank.html); LightGBM rank_xendcg objective (Bruch et al., WSDM 2020); CatBoost QuerySoftMax / MRR metric
- What: With one relevant candidate per query, average precision equals reciprocal rank. XGBoost's rank:map is therefore LambdaMART weighted by the change in MRR. Listwise softmax cross-entropy (ListNet with a one-hot target) is a proven bound on MRR and NDCG under binary relevance. Its output is a within-list probability that item 1 can use directly. LightGBM lambdarank optimises NDCG, whose gain at rank 10 (0.29) is ~3x the MRR credit (0.10). Gradient therefore goes into deep swaps that MRR barely rewards.
- Evidence: Bruch et al. 2019: the ListNet loss bounds MRR and NDCG, which explains why it does well on binary labels. Donmez et al. 2009: LambdaRank finds a local optimum for NDCG, MAP and MRR at 99% confidence, and with enough data, training on the target measure gives the best accuracy. MRR yields the fewest 'effective pairs', which is why XGBoost implements MAP and NDCG but not MRR. We found no metabolomics-specific benchmark. Expect small gains (roughly +0.002 to +0.01).
- Fit: Cheap A/B on existing out-of-fold features and HL-val: (a) LightGBM custom objective, softmax CE per query (grad p - y, hess p(1-p)); (b) objective=rank_xendcg; (c) XGBoost rank:map with lambdarank_pair_method=topk and lambdarank_num_pair_per_sample ~25-30; (d) lambdarank with lambdarank_truncation_level=25-30. Ensemble by averaging per-query softmax probabilities, not ranks. The main payoff is calibrated within-source probabilities for item 1. CPU only. Eligible.

### [ranking-ml] Documentation priors that are category-specific and selection-aware (MetFrag / PubChemLite metadata scoring)

- Source: Ruttkies et al. 2016 J Cheminform 8:3, 'MetFrag relaunched' (PMC4732001); Schymanski et al. 2021 J Cheminform 13:19, 'PubChemLite meets MetFrag' (PMC7938590); Blazenovic et al. 2017 J Cheminform 9:32, 'database boosting is needed to achieve 93% accuracy' (PMC5445034)
- What: Metadata priors are the strongest re-ranker for well-known compounds: reference and patent counts, membership in DBs, and category-specific annotation counts. Two conditions apply. (i) The prior must describe the population the test was drawn from. Here that is compounds measured by Enveda with no public spectra, so a prior learned on held-out training structures, which do have spectra, is over-confident. (ii) The prior should only break near-ties, so spectral evidence can overrule it for less-documented isomers. Category-specific counts beat total popularity.
- Evidence: MetFrag on 473 standards: top-1 6% -> 71% (PubChem) and 22% -> 89% (ChemSpider) after adding refs/patents + RT. CASMI 2016 (208 challenges): fragmentation alone gave 11.5-33.7% top-1, vs 78.8% with metadata. Blazenovic et al.: MetFrag in silico alone 53/208 (25%); DB presence + random order 119 (57%); ChemSpider-ID order alone 106 (51%); best combination 181 (87%). PubChemLite: literature + patent counts alone gave 58% top-1 in full PubChem and 70% in PubChemLite. A category-specific AgroChemScore raised this to 79.2%, and adding MS/MS gave 90.6%. Generic counts still misranked isomers: secbutylazine came 3rd, behind better-documented terbutylazine and propazine. Public lineage: pop = f(depositors, vendors, MeSH, synonyms) with lambda 0.15-0.25, plus a +1.0 NP bonus for COCONUT/LOTUS/NPAtlas membership.
- Fit: We already have doc priors and vendor features, so the lever is conditioning, not more features. Fit the prior's coefficients on simulated class-2 queries whose answers are restricted or re-weighted to look like the test population. That means in COCONUT and absent from GNPS/MassBank/MoNA releases. Let item 3's EM set the strength. Use category counts instead of totals: number of NP-DB memberships, LOTUS organism count, presence in NP vendor libraries, ChEBI role. Add interactions with the spectral margin so the prior acts mainly when the FP/GLACIER evidence is tied. Targets the COCONUT-not-train gap (0.208 vs 0.252) and the PubChem tier. Eligible: PubChem counts, COCONUT, LOTUS, NPAtlas. CPU.

### [ranking-ml] Test-set (transductive) priors: molecular-network consensus re-ranking (NAP/MetFusion), optionally taxonomic co-occurrence

- Source: da Silva et al. 2018 PLoS Comput Biol 14:e1006089, 'Propagating annotations of molecular networks using in silico fragmentation' (PMC5927460); Gerlich & Neumann 2013 J Mass Spectrom (MetFusion); ChemWalker, Bioinformatics 2023 (PMC9991053); Rutz et al. 2019 Front Plant Sci 10:1329, taxonomically informed scoring (PMC6824209)
- What: Build a spectral-similarity network over the hidden test spectra plus the train library. Re-rank each molecule's candidates by structural similarity to the library hits of its spectral neighbours (Fusion) and to the top-k candidates of neighbouring test molecules (Consensus). Optionally add co-occurrence in the same organisms (LOTUS) with confidently identified test molecules. The test set's own structure then acts as a population prior.
- Evidence: NAP on 5,467 [M+H]+ spectra that had network neighbours, searched against a 367k-structure bio DB. Mean rank of the correct structure: 14.7 for MetFrag alone, 4.7 with Fusion, 6.3 with Consensus. Median: 5 -> 1 and 2. First-rank hits: +29.0% (Fusion), +19.8% (Consensus). Taxonomic scoring on 2,107 GNPS spectra with a known biological source raised top-1 for ISDB-DNP from 10.2% to 71.7%, for SIRIUS from 46.3% to 71.6%, and for MS-Finder from 8.5% to 25.9%.
- Fit: Add as stage-2 features: max Tanimoto of a candidate to the top-3 candidates of test neighbours with cosine > 0.7, weighted by each neighbour's item-2 confidence; and library-neighbour Fusion similarity. Pays off only if the ~400 test molecules include analog series or shared source organisms. Measure in the notebook the fraction of test molecules with a neighbour above 0.7 and gate the feature on it. To train it, simulate held-out query sets drawn from Enveda batches in train.parquet, so network density matches. CPU-cheap; eligible. The taxonomic version needs the test organisms to be inferable from class-1 hits: speculative, lowest priority.
